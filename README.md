# Mentora

**Where teachers and students learn together.**

Teachers run classes, make quizzes, flashcards and study guides grounded in
real sources or their own files, share them with a class or a group, and watch
the results arrive live. They can also schedule a **live lesson** that Astra,
the tutor, teaches out loud to a group.
Students join with a link, play each set one question at a time with a study
buddy cheering them on, and see their strengths and the skills to practise
next. **Parents** connect through their child's invitation and follow along.

![A student's home: their buddy, their streak, and the work their teachers shared](docs/screenshots/student-home.png)

## For students

A playful, big-text view built for children: rounded display fonts, a text
size control everywhere they read, and animation that respects reduced motion.

**Three looks, picked from the student's year.**
- **Year 1–3** get bright bobbing tiles, a star path, confetti for every right
  answer, big playful words, and *Read it to me* in Astra's warm voice.
- **Year 4–6** play a quest: a map grid, a rocket riding the progress bar,
  points on each right answer and a rank at the end.
- **Form 1–5** get the clean, focused look.

Students practise what their teachers and family send, and what their buddy
makes from the bits they found tricky. They don't make sets themselves.

**Five study buddies** — Kiko, Tompok, Ollie, Momo and Rimau — each with their
own voice, moves, tricks and sounds (a babble in their own pitch under every
speech bubble). They greet, cheer right answers, comfort wrong ones, and
coach: halfway and last-one cheers, a nudge when a child is stuck, and a way
to think after two misses. The buddy goes with a student to **every page**,
knowing their progress, schedule, strengths and what needs practice, and
says the useful part: what is due, the skill to practise, a tip for doing
better at quizzes, flashcards, study guides and live lessons. **My results** is written for a child to read: the
buddy's one-line summary, recent scores, and skills sorted into *Got it*,
*Getting there* and *Let's practise*.

**A buddy that grows as you learn.** Every student's buddy starts as a tiny
baby animal (big head, big eyes, a little wobbly) and grows through four
stages, newborn to grown, learning new moves at each, with its own trick
last. Only learning makes it grow: finishing work, three stars, a badge, a
skill mastered, coming back each day. Growing up is a full-screen moment,
never in the middle of a question. New buddy friends are earned too, one
for every three badges. Parents see how grown their child's buddy is
([`docs/features/052-growing-buddy.md`](docs/features/052-growing-buddy.md)).

## Study guides

A topic taught part by part, from real sources, with a picture in every part.
Each part is written three times — **Simpler**, **Just right** and
**Challenge** — so a whole class reads the same lesson at a pace that fits
each child. Words to know open their meaning and their Malay, **Read it to me**
reads a part aloud with the sentence lighting up, and every part ends with a
quick check the teacher sees live.

![A study guide's cover: a big question, the parts pinned like polaroids, how long it takes](docs/screenshots/study-guide-cover.png)

## Play and Learn games

**Every game is a mission, told aloud.** It opens on a briefing (a radar
sweeping, *INCOMING MISSION* typed out), then the story is read by the
tutor's voice over a quiet, tense music bed, the words lighting up as they
are said. The child accepts the mission with a stamp, and every island, what
it taught and the ending are told as they appear. Dramatic, but never loud;
one sound switch, remembered
([`docs/features/053-mission-narration.md`](docs/features/053-mission-narration.md)).

| | |
|---|---|
| ![The mission briefing: a radar, INCOMING MISSION, the game's name and Start the mission](docs/screenshots/game-briefing.png) | ![The opening read aloud, the word being said lit, and Accept the mission](docs/screenshots/game-told-aloud.png) |

A game on any topic, made by a teacher or parent in a minute or two. It is a
treasure map of islands, one idea each, told as a short story with the
student's own buddy, and it opens a different way each time: a comic, a
video call, a letter in a bottle, a news flash or a postcard. On each island
the student plays a round in one of Mentora's eight templates, ending with a
**Boss Battle**:

- **Sort It Out**, **Line Up** and **Match Up**;
- **The Lab** (sliders that change a formula's output);
- **Spot the Mistake**;
- **Detective Case**;
- **Escape Room**.

The AI writes only the content, checked like a quiz, and fits a template to
each idea. The year (Year 1–6, Form 1–5) sets the size, the words, the help
and which templates are offered.

|---|---|
| ![The treasure map: islands on a winding path, the next one glowing with the buddy on it, the rest under fog](docs/screenshots/game-map.png) | ![Line Up: steps tapped into order; a wrong one is answered by the buddy with why](docs/screenshots/game-round.png) |
| **The map**: one idea per island; the fog lifts as you go. | **A round**: every move answered at once — a wrong one with why. |
| ![Learn it: the story beat, then the one idea big with its picture, and what to do](docs/screenshots/game-learn.png) | ![What you discovered: stars, the checked explanation, and the story moving on](docs/screenshots/game-discovery.png) |
| **Learn it**: the idea first, read aloud. | **What you discovered**: stars, why it works, and the story goes on. |

- **Every move is answered at once.** Every card, step, pair, line, clue,
  door and question has its own *why*, which the buddy says after a wrong
  move.
- **Revision games** use the game's own questions:
  - **Streak Ladder**, from the finish screen;
  - **Tug-of-War**, two teams on the teacher's class screen.
- **Always built.** A round that breaks its rules is played as a Match Up
  of the island's pairs, so a game never fails to build.
- **Never stuck.** **Show me** plays one right move once a student is
  stuck, and hints cost a few points. Students earn stars and points, and
  *Islands to clear again* replays only the ones missed.
- **Make it** in the app or from the chat: pick how it plays (or let Mentora
  choose), the world and the story. **Play it first**, edit any piece, then
  share it with a class (with a best-points **leaderboard** if you want one)
  or send it home. Results show the pieces most often wrong.

See [`docs/features/050-play-and-learn.md`](docs/features/050-play-and-learn.md).

## Live lessons

A teacher plans a lesson in four steps (who, what, how, after) and Mentora
writes it part by part. The teacher listens to every part in Astra's voice,
edits or rewrites what they want, and approves it. Then it goes on the
schedule of the whole class, or of the one group it is for.

- **Everyone hears the same sentence at the same time**, with the words
  lighting up, the key idea on screen and a star per part.
- **The lobby** opens ten minutes before. Each classmate floats in as their
  own buddy. Students never message each other.
- **Ask Astra, out loud or typed.** Questions queue in turn. Astra reads each
  one out, thanks the student by name and answers for the room.
- **The teacher's room** shows who is here, the question queue and the
  transcript as it is said. The teacher can pause, skip a part or end the
  lesson.
- **Afterwards**, the teacher gets a summary: attendance, every question and
  Astra's answer, the quick checks and the quiz average. Students get their
  notes. If the teacher chose one, a quiz made from the lesson is shared with
  the group.

See [`docs/features/037-live-lessons.md`](docs/features/037-live-lessons.md)
and [`docs/features/038-live-room.md`](docs/features/038-live-room.md).

### My day: the Daily Quest

A student's home opens on **Today's Quest**: five quick questions from their
own work, review first, about five minutes a day. Beside it, a **streak**
counted in Malaysian days, with freezes that cover a missed day; a **skill
garden** where each skill is a plant that droops when it is due for review;
and a **class quest**, everyone's quests adding up to a weekly goal and a
buddy party, with no one ranked
([`docs/features/055-daily-quest.md`](docs/features/055-daily-quest.md)).

![A student's home: today's quest with the streak, the skill garden with plants needing water, and the class quest](docs/screenshots/my-day.png)

### Second chance, at your level

A wrong answer opens **Let's look at it together**: why the right answer is
right at the level the child picks (Simpler, Just right or Challenge), a
worked example stepped through, then **one like it** to try. Right on the
second try, the buddy cheers and grows; the score stays as it was. A
teacher can share a quiz in **Test mode** to switch it off
([`docs/features/056-second-chance.md`](docs/features/056-second-chance.md)).

![A second chance: the question and its answer, the level switch, why, and a worked example](docs/screenshots/second-chance.png)

### Learn, practise, play

A study guide is a journey: each part ends with a one-minute game made from
its own words (a Match Up, and a Line Up when it describes a process), the
buddy walks the trail as parts are finished, and finishing unlocks the
guide's Boss Battle and a short quiz. No extra AI calls
([`docs/features/057-learn-practise-play.md`](docs/features/057-learn-practise-play.md)).

![The end of a study guide: the journey with every part lit, and the Boss Battle and quiz unlocked](docs/screenshots/guide-journey.png)

### Hands and voice

Games can be dragged everywhere, with a magnet that pulls each piece in and
a knock when it lands. **Say It** (Year 1–3, English and Malay) has children
read lines aloud and lights each word as it is heard. **Explain why** (Form
1–5) sometimes asks "Tell me why" after a right answer and answers the
reasoning in a line
([`docs/features/058-hands-and-voice.md`](docs/features/058-hands-and-voice.md)).

### Help at home

A parent taps a skill their child finds hard and gets what it means in
plain words, five minutes to do together at home, and **Send a challenge**:
a short quiz on just that skill, which their child's buddy brings them. In
English or Bahasa Melayu. Parents can also send preset **cheers** the buddy
acts out. Teachers get **Practice for N** under any red skill on an
assignment's heatmap: practice made from the assignment's own sources, for
just those students
([`docs/features/054-help-at-home.md`](docs/features/054-help-at-home.md)).

- **My materials.** Upload the worksheets, chapters and notes you teach from
  once (PDF, Word, PowerPoint or text), or **take a photo** of a page or the
  board. Make quizzes, flashcards, study guides and live lessons from them.
  Every item cites your file. The web fills gaps only if you ask it to.
- **The coverage map.** It shows how much of the year's syllabus each class
  has covered, month by month and topic by topic, with the class's score on
  each. It can draft the syllabus, suggest a plan for the rest of the year,
  and send a progress report home as a private link. Then it keeps nudging:
  the teacher sees the next topics to teach, and each student sees the class
  work they still have to finish.
- **Make it by asking.** In the chat, *"Make me a quiz on fractions for Year 4
  maths"* starts the quiz (or a game, a deck or a guide), and a card in the
  chat follows it until it's ready.
  It works out the subject from the topic, and asks only for what it cannot
  guess (usually the year).
- **Made for you.** When a student finds a shared quiz hard, Mentora makes
  them a short practice set on exactly the skills they missed.
- **Work in the background.** Making a set or recording a lesson carries on
  while you do something else. A tray beside the bell shows its progress,
  and the bell rings when it is ready.
- **Messages with families.** Write to the parents of any student in your
  classes, from **Messages** or the student list. Replies arrive live, and
  the envelope beside the bell counts what is waiting.

## For parents

A parent signs up on their own, or from the link their child sends from
**Settings → My family**. A parent who signed up alone connects by typing the
child's 6-letter code.

- **Linked to the child's teachers automatically.** The parent sees the
  child's classes, teachers and schedule. The teacher sees which students
  have a parent connected, and the two can message each other.
- **Only the parent can disconnect.** The child is told when a parent
  connects, and sees who is connected.
- **Everything the child does**, updated live as they work. Each child's
  page has six tabs: an overview of the week, all their work (past due
  first), results by skill, their practice, their live-lesson schedule, and
  each class with what it covers. Any finished quiz opens answer by answer.
- **Make something and send it home.** Parents make quizzes, flashcards,
  study guides and games from a topic or their own files. They send them to
  their child, with a due date if they like. The child finds them under
  *From home*, and the parent hears the score when it's done.
- **An instant alert when work goes past due**, whether a teacher or a
  parent set it. It arrives once, says where the work came from, and opens
  the child's work.
- **A chat of their own**, for helping at home: plain words, practical
  ideas, and it knows their children by name and year and what each is
  practising. It never shows a score or another child. There is no studio.
- **Messages with the teacher.** Write to each class's teacher about how the
  child is doing, homework, or anything to clarify. Replies arrive live
  ([`048`](docs/features/048-parent-teacher-messages.md)). The child never
  sees these messages.

See [`docs/features/042-parents.md`](docs/features/042-parents.md).

## Features

- **Four roles.** Admin, teacher, student and parent, each with its own
  signup, home and permissions — decided in one policy file and enforced by
  the API, not by hiding buttons.
- **Classes and groups.** Invite links and codes, join requests, groups, and
  work shared with a whole class or one group.
- **Grounded generation.** Quizzes, flashcards and study guides are written
  from the teacher's own files, sources the app searched and read, or both.
  Every answer is checked against them and the sources are shown. Malaysian
  Year and Form grades, in English, Malay, Tamil, Chinese or Bengali.
- **Live lessons with Astra.** Written, voiced and taught to a group on a
  schedule, with spoken questions answered by name. English only for now.
- **Study guides.** A topic taught part by part at three reading levels, with
  pictures, words to know in Malay, read-aloud, a concept map, a check after
  every part, and a printable handout.
- **Play and Learn games.** A treasure map of islands in a story that opens
  five different ways, one idea each, played in eight templates of Mentora's
  own (from Sort It Out and The Lab to Detective Case, Escape Room and a
  final Boss Battle), sized to the year. Streak Ladder and class-screen
  Tug-of-War for revision. Always built, in a minute or two.
- **Practice made for each student.** When a shared quiz is tricky, Mentora
  makes a short practice set on exactly the skills missed. Students practise;
  only teachers and parents make sets.
- **Three looks by year.** Quizzes and flashcards play differently for Year
  1–3, Year 4–6 and the Forms, picked from the student's year. Teachers and
  parents preview a set in its year's look. Year 1–3 get *Read it to me* in
  the tutor's voice.
- **Results and skills.** Per-student, per-question and per-skill results,
  a score spread, and a student's own strengths and next steps.
- **Coverage and reports home.** A syllabus map per class and private
  progress links for families.
- **Parent–teacher messages.** Live, one conversation per parent, teacher
  and child, closed the moment the link ends.
- **Real time everywhere.** Notifications, progress while a quiz is being
  taken, leaderboards, class lists and join requests update without a reload.
- **Study buddies, badges and streaks** to keep students coming back. Buddies
  start as babies and grow as the student learns; new buddy friends are
  earned with badges.
- **Guardrails for students.** A student in distress is answered with care
  and pointed to a trusted adult and Malaysian helplines; personal details are
  redacted; flagged moments go to a review queue. English and Malay.
- **Admin console.** Platform overview, user management, the safety queue and
  content oversight.
- **Accessible by default.** Text size from small to biggest, playful,
  classic or easy-read fonts, reduced motion, light and dark themes.
- **Every model call traced.** With Langfuse keys set, each chat turn and
  each quiz, guide or game build is a trace in Langfuse, its stages as steps,
  with tokens, timings and cost. Builds' text is kept; chats only as counts.
  `make langfuse` runs Langfuse locally
  ([`docs/features/051-observability.md`](docs/features/051-observability.md)).

> **Students have no open chat for now, and don't make sets.** They take
> their class's work, work sent from home, and the practice made for them;
> every chat and set-making route refuses them.
>
> **The studio is switched off.** No one makes posters, slides, AI-coded
> games, websites or apps, in the chat or anywhere else. (Play and Learn games
> are a learning kind, not a studio artifact, and are on: `GAMES_ENABLED`.) Ones already made can still
> be opened, downloaded and shared. See
> [`docs/features/025-roles-and-signup.md`](docs/features/025-roles-and-signup.md).

## Quick start

Docker is the only prerequisite.

```bash
git clone https://github.com/khaled-saiful-islam/mentora.git && cd mentora
cp .env.example .env          # then set LLM_API_KEY
make up
```

Open <http://localhost:8300> and sign in with **admin / admin**.

`make up` builds the images, waits for Postgres, runs migrations, seeds the
admin account and starts everything. To try it with a class already full of
work, run `make demo` — it seeds a demo teacher, three students and shared
quizzes and flashcards, plus Aina's mum with work she sent home, and prints
their sign-ins.

| Container | Port | |
|---|---|---|
| `mentora-frontend` | `8300` | The web app (nginx, proxies `/api`) |
| `mentora-backend` | `8301` | FastAPI — docs at `/api/docs` |
| `mentora-db` | `8302` | PostgreSQL 16 |

`make dev` serves the frontend with hot reload on `8303`.

> **Change `SEED_ADMIN_PASSWORD` and `JWT_SECRET` before deploying anywhere.**
> The app refuses to start with the shipped defaults when `APP_ENV=production`.

## Production

Mentora is live at **https://mentora.stream**: one AWS Lightsail server in
Singapore running this same Compose stack, with Cloudflare in front for DNS
and HTTPS. After pushing to `main`:

```bash
make deploy            # back up the database, pull main, rebuild, wait until healthy
```

`make deploy` needs `.env.deploy` (copy `.env.deploy.example`). On the server,
`scripts/backup-db.sh` dumps the database on demand; it also runs nightly.

How it is set up, and why, is in
[`docs/features/049-production-deployment.md`](docs/features/049-production-deployment.md).

## Configuration

Everything is set in `.env`; [`.env.example`](.env.example) documents every
setting. The ones you are most likely to change:

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| `LLM_BASE_URL`, `LLM_API_KEY`, `LLM_MODEL` | Any OpenAI-compatible model endpoint |
| `LEARNING_MODEL` | The model that writes quizzes and flashcards (empty uses `LLM_MODEL`) |
| `SERPAPI_KEY` | Web search, for grounded sets |
| `SPEECH_MODEL`, `SPEECH_VOICE`, `TRANSCRIBE_MODEL` | Astra's voice, and hearing students' spoken questions, in live lessons |
| `MATERIALS_PER_OWNER`, `MATERIAL_MAX_BYTES` | How many files a teacher or parent can keep in My materials, and how big each can be |
| `PARENT_SETS_PER_DAY` | How many quizzes, decks and guides a parent may make each day |
| `MODERATION_ENABLED` | The student guardrails |
| `PUBLIC_BASE_URL` | Where invite and share links point — set it for any real deployment |
| `LANGFUSE_PUBLIC_KEY`, `LANGFUSE_SECRET_KEY`, `LANGFUSE_HOST` | Trace every model call in Langfuse; `LANGFUSE_CONTENT` says whose words are sent |

```
make up        build, migrate, seed, start, print the login
make demo      seed a demo class with work to try
make down      stop, keep the database
make dev       hot reload on both sides
make logs      follow logs
make test      backend pytest with coverage, then frontend vitest

```

## How it is built

- **Backend:** FastAPI, Python 3.12, SQLAlchemy 2.0 async, Alembic, Pydantic v2,
  PostgreSQL 16. Logic never imports FastAPI, and a test enforces it.
- **Frontend:** React 18, TypeScript (strict), Vite, Tailwind v4 with theme
  tokens only, Motion for animation, Phosphor icons.
- **Extensible by design.** Model providers, tools, prompt sources, artifact
  kinds, learning kinds, badge rules, safety screens and domain-event
  reactions are each a protocol plus a registry: adding one is a new file and
  one registry line.
- **Live updates** go over one server-sent event stream per signed-in person.

[`CLAUDE.md`](CLAUDE.md) is the guide to working in the codebase, and
[`docs/features/`](docs/features) has one document per feature — what it does,
how it works, how to extend it and its known limits. [`PLAN.md`](PLAN.md) is
the roadmap.
